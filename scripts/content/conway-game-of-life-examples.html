These live examples show three kinds of behaviour: still lifes, oscillators, and a travelling glider. Each board has 6 rows and 6 columns at 4× zoom. Move your pointer over a board to add living cells and change the pattern; reload the page to restore all examples. Opposite edges connect, so the glider can leave one side and return from the other.

Small patterns in Conway’s Game of Life
PatternWhat to watch

Block

Live Block example. Enable JavaScript to interact.

Still life · 4 living cells. A 2×2 square stays unchanged. Every living cell has three living neighbours, and no empty cell has exactly three.

Blinker

Live Blinker example. Enable JavaScript to interact.

Oscillator · period 2. A 1×3 horizontal line becomes a 3×1 vertical line, then returns. Only the middle cell survives each change; the two end cells are replaced.

Glider

Live Glider example. Enable JavaScript to interact.

Spaceship · 4 phases. Five living cells change shape and move one cell diagonally every four generations. On this wrapping 6×6 board, the undisturbed glider returns to its starting position after 24 generations.

Beehive

Live Beehive example. Enable JavaScript to interact.

Still life · 6 living cells. A hollow shape remains unchanged. Its living cells survive while the empty centre stays empty.

Toad

Live Toad example. Enable JavaScript to interact.

Oscillator · period 2. Two offset rows of three cells open into a wider shape, then close again. The pattern repeats every two generations.

Boat

Live Boat example. Enable JavaScript to interact.

Still life · 5 living cells. This small asymmetric shape stays unchanged. Like the glider, it has five living cells, but their arrangement produces stillness instead of travel.
