Accountia is a web-based invoicing and accounting product originally conceived for businesses in Iraq and the wider Middle East. The initial brief focused on invoices, customers, products, and basic accounting, with support for right-to-left languages and businesses working across currencies.

I worked as Lead Designer, bringing a UX and product design approach grounded in my technical background. My role covered translating product requirements into interface designs, developing a reusable Figma component library,  and designing and implementing the public website in WordPress.
 

Turning requirements into screens

 

I worked closely with the Product Owner to turn ideas and business requirements into concrete screens. These designs became the basis for development tickets, giving the team a visual reference for the functionality they needed to build.

The challenge was making connected financial information understandable across lists, forms, detail views, and dialogs. Customers, products, invoices, payments, and accounting records needed to work together within a consistent interface. My design work covered desktop and mobile layouts, including dashboards, sales and invoice management, product and contact records, payment accounts, and the general ledger.

From the website to the application

Evolving an existing visual direction

 

The starting point was an early set of screens created by another designer. I adopted that visual direction and developed it into a broader UI kit and design system, retaining recognisable elements while extending the interface to support more complex workflows.

The early invoice screen already established the purple and gold palette, light surfaces, and status colours. In the later designs, those foundations expanded into persistent sidebar navigation, clearer action groupings, and more detailed representations of payment status, invoice status, and sale type.

I rebuilt the Figma file three times during the project, carrying useful styles forward and refining how the system was organised. Each iteration reflected both the product’s changing needs and my growing understanding of component-based design.

Building consistency beyond individual screens

 

The design system documented typography, colour roles, spacing, icons, buttons, inputs, tables, dialogs, and reusable layout patterns. It also addressed product-specific details such as monetary values, currency display, record identifiers, and the distinction between actions that navigate elsewhere and actions that open a dialog.

This work gave me a shared foundation for designing new screens and revisiting existing ones. Instead of treating every page as a separate composition, I could develop related workflows through a common set of components and rules.

The website and collaboration

Alongside the application, I designed the landing page and implemented it myself in WordPress. The website introduced the product through its main workflows, using interface previews to explain invoicing, payment tracking, reporting, and resource management.

Our design team consisted of two people: myself and junior designer @Banaz. She supported the website’s translation work across multiple languages, including Arabic, while I led the design and website implementation.

 


 

What the project taught me


As a self-taught designer with a technical background, Accountia was an opportunity to develop my visual and systems thinking through sustained product work. The repeated evolution of the Figma library is an important part of that story: it shows how my approach matured alongside the interface.

Backend architecture and database structure were outside my responsibility. From my perspective, the amount of bespoke API work became a constraint on delivery speed. That experience reinforced the importance of discussing interface requirements, data relationships, and implementation effort together early in the process.

My contribution brought the original visual direction into a wider product interface, supported by a reusable design system and a website I implemented myself.
